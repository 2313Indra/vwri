Introduction to VWRI

The Village Water Resilience Index (VWRI) is a composite, indicator-based framework for assessing how resilient a village's water system is — covering groundwater and surface-water conditions, supply infrastructure, drinking-water quality, exposure to climate variability, and the socio-economic capacity of the community to cope with water stress.

The framework evaluates village-level water resilience across five pillars (“Realms”) built from 34 field-measurable indicators: Hydrological, Infrastructure, Water Quality, Climate, and Socio-economic. Each indicator is scored on a 5-tier scale (Very Low to Very High), aggregated into a Realm Score, and combined into a single Composite VWRI using fixed Realm weights.

The Five Realms of Resilience

A balanced multi-dimensional assessment drawing on 34 indicators with fixed realm weights summing to 1.00.

Hydrological

Weight: 0.30 (8 indicators)

Groundwater condition, recharge, source density and availability of water at the village level.

Infrastructure

Weight: 0.20 (9 indicators)

Coverage, storage, reliability and physical condition of water-supply infrastructure.

Water Quality

Weight: 0.15 (4 indicators)

Chemical and biological safety of drinking water and its stability across seasons.

Climate

Weight: 0.15 (7 indicators)

Rainfall variability, drought exposure, warming trend and mitigation of water-related emissions.

Socio-economic

Weight: 0.20 (6 indicators)

Household income, dependency on shared water resources, migration and community governance.

Water Resilience Classification

Realm Scores (RS) and the final Composite VWRI are classified across five distinct resilience tiers:

Realm Score Range (RS) / Composite VWRI Water Resilience Level
$\text{RS} \le 1.5$ Very Low resilience
$1.5 < \text{RS} \le 2.5$ Low resilience
$2.5 < \text{RS} \le 3.5$ Moderate resilience
$3.5 < \text{RS} \le 4.5$ High resilience
$\text{RS} > 4.5$ Very High resilience
